# Impact review

Review before rewriting. Impact means the idea matters more to the reader; it does not mean more dramatic prose, stronger adjectives, artificial certainty, provocative titles, shorter paragraphs or more headings.

Load `STYLE.md` and `VOICE.md` from the user-named profile or the first available profile at `.writing/`, `.writing-profile/`, `~/.writing/` or `~/.codex/writing-profile/`. Continue without a profile when none exists.

## Evaluate

1. Purpose: state in one sentence why the piece exists. Flag it when this cannot be done.
2. Audience: identify what this reader needs to understand, decide or do.
3. Surprise: identify what becomes newly visible or newly believable.
4. Evidence: find the claims carrying the most weight and classify them as supported, plausible but unsupported, anecdotal, asserted or needing research.
5. Consequence: if the piece is right, what changes in thought, design, ownership, practice or decision?
6. Memory: what useful idea will remain twenty-four hours later?

For design documents and proposals, treat a clearer trade-off or more credible decision as impact even when there is no provocative thesis.

## Respond

Start with what already works. Name the biggest missed opportunity. Suggest three to five surgical changes, then an optional deeper change only if structure or purpose genuinely needs it.

Show revisions for the highest-value passages when helpful, preserving the profile's voice. Do not rewrite good prose merely to demonstrate activity. Do not fabricate evidence; mark verification work explicitly.
