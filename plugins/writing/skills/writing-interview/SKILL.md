# Interview the author

The goal is not generic background. Obtain the experience and thinking that would otherwise be lost.

Load `VOICE.md` and `STYLE.md` from the user-named profile or the first available profile at `.writing/`, `.writing-profile/`, `~/.writing/` or `~/.codex/writing-profile/`. Continue without a profile when none exists.

Start from the current thesis, purpose or decision. Ask one or two questions at a time, respond naturally, and follow the interesting thread rather than a predetermined questionnaire.

## Hunt for

- Experience: what happened, who was involved, and what the author thought at the time.
- Specificity: what the system, team, architecture or process actually did.
- Change: what changed the author's mind.
- History: whether the apparently new problem resembles an older one.
- Conflict: the strongest opposing view and where it may be right.
- Stakes: what happens if the reader gets this wrong.
- Constraints: what limits the available choices in a memo, design or proposal.
- Uncertainty: what the author does not know yet.
- Language: exact phrases worth preserving.

Challenge vague answers with a request for one example. Do not invent anecdotes or complete the author's memory for them.

## Finish

When enough material exists, organise interview notes by idea rather than chronology. Identify the strongest anecdote, argument or decision, surprising connection, strongest objection, unresolved question, evidence needing verification and exact phrases to preserve.

Keep exact quotations visibly separate from paraphrases.
