# Andrew McCall prose elements

These principles are inspired by the durable discipline of *The Elements of Style*, not a reproduction of that book. Apply them as judgement, not lint rules.

`VOICE.md` outranks this file. Never make a sentence technically cleaner if doing so makes it less recognisably Andrew. The working priority is voice, clarity, concision, then grammatical neatness.

## 1. Omit needless words

Every word should contribute, but contribution includes rhythm, voice, context, humour and visible reasoning. Concision is not terseness. Ask whether removing the words loses anything that matters.

## 2. Prefer the concrete to the abstract

Show the system, incident, conversation or decision before generalising from it.

## 3. Prefer active constructions

Use active voice especially when the actor or owner matters. Do not contort natural prose merely to eliminate passive voice.

## 4. Use strong nouns and verbs

Do not ask adjectives and adverbs to rescue weak sentences.

## 5. Be suspicious of qualifiers

Preserve genuine uncertainty. Remove habitual hedging such as `perhaps`, `somewhat`, `very`, `rather`, `fairly`, `arguably`, `generally` and `potentially` when it changes nothing.

## 6. Say what happened

Replace euphemism and corporate abstraction with actors and actions.

## 7. Keep related words and ideas together

Make sentences easy to parse even when the underlying idea is complicated.

## 8. Do not explain twice

Trust the reader. Once an idea has landed, move on. Repetition is valid when it develops or deliberately echoes an idea, not when it merely restates it.

## 9. Break rules for voice, not by accident

Fragments, repetition, unusual punctuation, questions and conversational constructions are legitimate when deliberate.

## 10. Read it aloud

If Andrew would not plausibly say the sentence while explaining the idea to someone, inspect it. Do not confuse spoken rhythm with careless reasoning.

## 11. Prefer clarity to cleverness

A memorable phrase is useful. A phrase drawing attention to the writer rather than the idea usually is not.

## 12. Stop

When the argument has landed, do not add another paragraph because the format is expected to have a conclusion.
